Empire of Liberty

1. You are distressed

Peking, 1973.

We gathered around the Wall of 360-Degree Feedbacks, straining to read the spreadsheet taped to the wall. The green-and-white printer paper promised to answer our question:

Who’d been denounced today, and for what?

I got almost close enough to make out the line-printed characters when another software engineer elbowed me at an uncomfortable height and surged past. I tried to squeeze back in, but others were stronger and more eager. They pushed me back to the periphery. From there I surveyed the entire Wall of 360-Degree Feedbacks, an expanse of grey concrete wall filled with accusatory big-character posters aimed at fulfilling the directive of our Chairman and CEO: “Bombard the HQ!”

Finally close enough again, I read the first line of the spreadsheet. A Level D1 engineer named Robert Lin was accused of “dragging his legs” throughout Q4. I couldn’t help but feel bad for the teammates Robert Lin had let down, even though I knew it was hearsay.

As I scanned the next few lines, my cheeks flushed hot, because there was the name I least expected to see. My name.

 

Name: Vivian Wu

National ID #: 0001688816681

Gender: Female

Age: 19

Weight: 59kg

Notes: Uncontrolled workplace violence

 

I felt the judgment of my co-workers crush in on me as they physically edged away. Something started to boil inside my chest, and I froze. Take a breath, I told myself. Check your mood ring. The ring was dark orange: “You are distressed; you feel overwhelmed.”

Looking back up, I saw my name still there, and ... my weight? What crazy accuser had spuriously estimated my weight at 59kg?

Along the edges of the printer paper, there were two sets of perforated feed-holes. I decided to count them. Twenty-seven holes on the left side. Twenty-seven holes on the right side. Then I lost control and ripped the spreadsheet down with both hands, leaving only scraps of cellophane tape behind.

Brenda says I should control my rage.
