Empire of Liberty

15. This is not a promotion

Two weeks passed with only one incident of note.

I sat in the office of my HR representative, Cherry Xu and waited for her to arrive so that we could talk about it.

“Vivian Wu, right?” she said.

“Yes.”

Cherry Xu's hair was pulled back so tight that not a single strand escaped—it was the most painful-looking hairstyle I’d ever seen. She punched a few keys on her terminal.

“Hmm,” she said. “The system’s down.”

“Oh,” I said.

“You’re here to disclose a romantic workplace relationship, right?” She fished out a paper form from a drawer and slid it across the desk. Strangely, it included an outline of a female body.

“I’m not here for that,” I said. “I need a new terminal.”

“So, you’re the one,” she said. “You shoved your terminal and cracked the screen.”

There was a good reason why that happened, but Cherry Xu didn’t ask for it. Instead, she wrote a name on a piece of paper and handed it to me.

Gerald Yang.

“Three of your four cross-functional managers aren’t happy with you, so I’m assigning you to work with Level D3 Contractor Gerald Yang, from the Ministry of Defense.”

Is this a promotion? I wondered.

“This is not a promotion,” Cherry Xu said, tapping on her keyboard. “Okay, the system’s back up, and here’s your file. Hmm.”

She hit a couple more keys.

“You’re missing an emergency contact,” she said.

“What’s an emergency contact?” I asked.

“It’s who we should call if you have an accident.”

“Like a doctor?”

“No. Like a family member or friend who’s available to help you.”

My younger brother was family, but he was fighting in Vietnam and certainly not available. I missed him. After my debacle with the national college entrance exam, my brother rode his bicycle to the hospital to pick me up. He carried my backpack on the way home and didn’t ask me a single question the whole way. He was drafted the following spring.

“It’s a mandatory field,” Cherry Xu said distractedly.

Her Strollman buzzed with an incoming audio clip. As she played the message through her metal-banded headphones, she tilted her head and gestured distractedly at me.

Where it said “emergency contact,” I typed Cherry Xu’s name.

In another field, I glimpsed the words “Uncontrolled workplace violence.”

What happened was this: one of my cross-functional managers was reading over my shoulder, and he tried to rest his hand on the back of my neck, so I automatically lashed out, but my screen got in the way, and it got broken.
