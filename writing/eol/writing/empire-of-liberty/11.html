Empire of Liberty

11. Soviet policeman

Oslo, 1976.

There is a hard knock at my front door. It can’t be a social call, because Norwegians don’t socialize much in winter, and besides, I don’t have friends.

I open the door and a heavy Soviet policeman looks me up and down. In thickly accented Norwegian, he asks if I speak Russian.

“English,” I say.

He grimaces and pushes past me, while I shut the door to keep the cold air out. Chunks of snow fall from the policeman’s heavy coat as he retrieves a notepad from his pocket. With a scratchy ball-point pen, he writes down a name.

I read it out loud.

“Brenda Mann,” I say. “I don’t remember any Brenda Mann.”
