Empire of Liberty

17. You can switch from eye to eye

“Maybe try some small talk with this guy,” Brenda said.

Every Sunday, I met Brenda at Monkey Hot Coffee. We worked on mathematics, mostly, but sometimes she wrote her advice into my notebooks. That day she wrote:

 

Try to understand what others are thinking or feeling.

Avoid being inadvertently blunt or rude.

Make eye contact.

 

“How about we start with eye contact?” she suggested.

I looked Brenda in the eye. I already knew her eye color was green, but it appeared that the green was actually a composite of discrete flakes of blue, yellow, and brown. And the green was brightest near the pupil, darkening near the outer edge.

I tried to understand what Brenda was thinking or feeling, but I drew a blank. Eventually, I realized I was only looking at her left eye. I switched to her right eye, then back to her left eye again. How often was I supposed to switch from eye to eye? I wondered.

“You can switch from eye to eye as often as you like,” Brenda said. “Eventually, you’ll do it naturally, without thinking.”

“Okay,” I said, a little unsure.

Mixed into the coffee aroma between us was an unfamiliar, pleasing smell of foreign soap.

 

Don’t get inappropriately close to others.

Stifle nervous laughter.

Tell jokes.

 

“How about this one?” she said, circling a phrase. “Getting inappropriately close to others?”

“Am I inappropriately close right now?” I asked.

“It’s fine because I’m comfortable,” Brenda said. “Can you tell that I’m comfortable?”

“No,” I said.

She leaned back and dismissed the question with a wave of her hand. With confidence, she announced that I would get the hang of it in no time.

“Now, tell me more about this Gerald Yang. What kind of work does he do?”
