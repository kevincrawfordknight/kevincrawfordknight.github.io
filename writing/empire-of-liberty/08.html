Empire of Liberty

8. You’re not feeling well

I thought about it and concluded that Sunny Li was no longer a friend …

And Cherry Xu was no friend either ...

So, I held my breath and projected a simple thought into Cherry Xu’s head: This meeting is over.

“This meeting is over,” she said.

“What?” said Krystal Zhang.

You have another meeting, I projected. You’re not feeling well.

“I’m not feeling well,” Cherry Xu said.

Around the circle, everyone’s mouths dropped open, and in the following silence, I got up and left the room.

Back when I thought Brenda believed in me, and wanted the best for me, she showed me how to control my extra-sensory action ability, what I came to call my “Projector.” I’d been accidentally projecting my whole life—accidentally manipulating people, I guess. Instead of developing normal social skills.

At least by now I’d learned to turn the Projector on and off.

It was bad to turn it on, according to the literature Brenda gave me, because it created “cognitive dissonance” in the target person, which meant they struggled to fit the new information into their belief network. It was untraceable, but the mind of the target person would eventually associate me with some vague form of unpleasantness, and they would start to avoid me. I guess you could say that stunted social skills and a lack of friends are side effects of the Projector.

But the chemical side effects were more serious: each time I used it, some part of my memory was lost. I tried to remember how many ceiling tiles there were in Cherry Xu’s office. I’d counted them and re-counted them, just to be sure. It was an even number …

Anyway, it’s better not to use the Projector on friends and classmates.

But sometimes you’re in a jam, I guess.
