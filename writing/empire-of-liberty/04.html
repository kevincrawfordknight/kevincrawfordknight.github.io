Empire of Liberty

4. Spreading false corporate gossip

Peking, 1973.

Cherry Xu, my HR representative at Alicent Technologies, scheduled a struggle session for two o’clock, prompted, no doubt, by my recent outburst at the Wall of 360-Degree Feedbacks. I’d had two such “incidents” before, and as she put it, one more will make three.

An obvious mathematical fact; Brenda says I take things too literally.

Simultaneous beeps chirped from two computer terminals, mine and Sunny Li’s. We pushed our chairs back and walked down the row of engineers to Cherry Xu’s office.

Sunny Li and I were friends since the day we arrived at Alicent. We ate dinner together many evenings, and she always looked out for me. Sunny Li was a project manager at the company. Or a product manager. I could never remember the difference.

During orientation week, Sunny Li gave me three rules:

1. Pay attention to what goes on.

2. Don’t draw attention to yourself.

3. Deflect attention onto others.

Division head Krystal Zhang was already talking with Cherry Xu. They introduced two other classmates, Carrie Hu and Edsger Wang. At Alicent, we were supposed to say “classmate” instead of “co-worker,” because of the nice, casual feeling it conveyed.

Only important people like Cherry Xu had offices at Alicent; the rest of us sat shoulder-to-shoulder at our computer terminals. Cherry Xu’s austere office gave the impression that her decorating time had been sucked up by dealing with problems we created. Her desk terminal had a fancy detached keyboard, instead of the usual built-in one, and its screen was covered by some kind of polarized film that prevented viewing at an angle. The office walls were bare concrete except for a framed color photograph of our company’s Chairman and CEO.

After we assembled, Cherry Xu asked us to push the conference table to one wall and push six wooden chairs into a circle. I looked at the faces around the room. No one was older than twenty-one, with the possible exception of Cherry Xu.

“Let’s start with the fact that we’re all lucky to be here,” Cherry Xu began, “given the ongoing disruptions in the labor market, and so forth.”

She pressed the red button on her Strollman cassette player to start recording the meeting. The empty audiocassette box on her desk advertised “60 minutes recording time,” which I took to be an upper bound on how long the struggle session would last. After that, the audiotape would be uploaded to the mainframe computer for speech recognition and storage.

I looked past Cherry Xu at the Chairman’s framed photo on the wall. Studying it, I thought I detected a faint smirk on his face. Had that smirk been secretly added by some low-level artist in Alicent’s Office of Corporate Identity? I imagined the unfortunate illustrator attending her own struggle session right now.

“Krystal Zhang is the senior manager in this meeting,” continued Cherry Xu, “so I will consult with her about the outcome. I mean, she’ll make the decision.”

Cherry Xu hesitated then, as if trying to remember something.

Krystal Zhang arrived at Alicent Technologies the same day as me and Sunny Li. The three of us were assigned to the same lunch table, and as strangers, we decided to stick together. During the first weeks of orientation, Krystal Zhang struggled a lot, unable to master the basic technical materials. I covered for her and answered her questions, but Sunny Li wasn’t as patient, finally decreeing that we couldn’t be friends with Krystal anymore. I kept answering her questions anyway, in the stairwell where Sunny Li couldn’t see. Krystal barely passed the orientation test with two points to spare.

Later, she became a manager, which made sense, because everyone knows that managers don’t need to know all the technical details.

“What’s this about?” Sunny Li demanded.

By bringing attention on herself, she’d broken her own rule number two. Now she was leaning back and forth, searching under her chair for the height adjustment knob.

Cherry Xu answered that Sunny Li had been accused of spreading false corporate gossip. We were to address the matter with innovation, agility, passion, and so forth, following the Chairman’s procedure.

With a nervous laugh, Cherry Xu corrected herself, saying that “procedure” was probably not the right word. During the Re-org, the Chairman famously declared that corporate procedures were the dead hand of the past, trying to maintain its grip on the living.

“This is bullshit,” Sunny Li declared.
