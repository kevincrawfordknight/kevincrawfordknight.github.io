Empire of Liberty

16. Cigarette?

I found Gerald Yang in a temporary office with bare concrete walls that were painted army-blue below chest level. On his desk was a single, neat pile of documents, and an ashtray. A locked briefcase sat on the floor beside the desk.

He wore a black suit and tie, and his hair was slicked back in the style of upper management everywhere. I guessed he was in his late twenties, around Brenda’s age.

“You’re from the Ministry of Defense,” I said.

“Sorry?” said Gerald Yang.

“Can you get my brother out of Vietnam?” I asked.

“Look, miss—”

“Miss?”

He leaned back in his chair and regarded me.

“Cigarette?” he offered.

“I don’t smoke,” I said.

“Why don’t we start with your name?”

“Vivian Wu.”

He opened the folder on top of his pile.

“Three people interviewed you for Alicent, and all rated you very highly. Why do you think they did that?”

“They asked questions, and I answered them.”

Gerald Yang wrote something down.
